Te Wai-i-Kukawaka

Two works. Two elements. One story of water, land, and the connections that endure.

Works by Bella Rose Waetford

2026 · Limited Edition Relief Prints

Te Wai-i-Kukawaka is grounded in our connection to whenua and Kurawaka, the sacred place where the first ira tangata was shaped from Papatūānuku.

The unaunahi flows through the series, representing creation, whakapapa and the generations that descend from our beginnings, from atua, to tupuna, to mokopuna.

The colours are drawn from pigments gifted by Papatūānuku and used across different toi and cultural practices. Kerewhenua, kōkōwai and ngarahu each carry the essence of whenua, ahi and transformation.

Te Wai-i-Kukawaka (Kōkōwai)

Bella Rose Waetford · 2026

Kōkōwai is the rich red ochre traditionally treasured in Māori toi, ceremony, and practice. Its deep earthy tones speak to whakapapa, identity, and the enduring connection between people and the land.

Displayed alongside Kekewhenua, this work forms part of a single series meditation on creation, pigment, and the stories carried through generations.

Limited Edition Relief Print · 4/40

Te Wai-i-Kukawaka (Kekewhenua)

Bella Rose Waetford · 2026

Kekewhenua draws attention to the whenua — the earth itself — as a source of life, identity, and belonging. The warm ochre palette speaks to the pigments gifted by Papatūānuku, carrying the essence of land and transformation.

Paired with Kōkōwai, the two works together honour the enduring relationship between wai, whenua, and the generations that carry these stories forward.

About the Artist

Bella Rose Waetford Ngāti Wai, Ngāti Hine

Bella Rose Waetford (Ngāti Wai, Ngāti Hine) is a multidisciplinary Māori ringatoi whose practice is centred on customary Māori arts, natural materials, and the intergenerational transmission of mātauranga.

Her creative journey began through the Toi Ngāpuhi Taiohi Taioha programme in 2023, where she discovered a deeper passion for toi Māori and customary practice. That same year she began studying whakairo at Te Wānanga o Aotearoa under the guidance of Ngaraiti Smith.

Through the mentorship of her aunty, Te Hemoata Henare, Bella Rose was encouraged to continue her studies at Maunga Kura Toi in 2024. Now in her final year, she has had the privilege of learning from Te Hemoata Henare (Raranga), Rariera Marino (Whakairo), and Lorraine King (Rauangi), whose guidance has deepened her understanding of mātauranga Māori and strengthened her confidence as a ringatoi.

She works within the mediums of uku, raranga, whakairo and visual arts, drawing upon the resources of the whenua and moana to create taonga that carry whakapapa, identity, and the pūrākau of her whānau and culture. Her work reflects a commitment to the revitalisation of Māori creative practices and to ensuring that mātauranga continues to be shared across generations.

She is passionate about kaupapa that bring people together through wānanga, collective learning, and community practice, believing that creativity is one of the strongest ways to nurture identity, belonging, and connection throughout whānau, hapū, and iwi across Te Tai Tokerau.
